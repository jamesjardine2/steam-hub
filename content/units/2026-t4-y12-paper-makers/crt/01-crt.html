Lesson 1: Paper Power

Year 1/2 STEAM | Unit: Stronger Together: Paper Makers | CRT plan | 45 minutes

No preparation needed. Everything is handed out by student helpers during the lesson. No screens are used.

This week each pair tests three paper shapes, and each student glues a record sheet into their STEAM journal. Journals are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To discover that changing the shape of paper can change how strong it is.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • fold and roll paper into different shapes.
  • test how many pebbles each shape can hold.
  • say which shape was strongest and why.

Read these aloud at the start and come back to them at the end.

How to do the activity

  • Gap: place two books flat on the table, one hand-span apart. Keep the gap the same for every test so it is fair.
  • Flat: lay a sheet across the gap like a bridge.
  • Fan: fold a sheet back and forth like a paper fan. The folds must run from book to book, not across the gap.
  • Tube: roll a sheet up and tape it closed.
  • Test: place pebbles gently, one at a time, in the middle. Count each one. Stop when the paper sags down to the table or collapses. That number is the score.
  • If pebbles roll off a tube, pile them on top, or count the pebble that falls as the last one.

Key words

strong, weak, shape, fold, roll, test, predict, weight

Lesson sequence

TimeStepWhat to do and say
0 to 3 minSettleStudents sit on the floor facing you. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud.
3 to 10 minHookHold up one sheet of paper. Ask: "Could this piece of paper hold up these pebbles?" Thumbs up or down. Lay it flat across a gap between two books and place one pebble in the middle. It sags. Say: "Paper is weak... or is it? Watch." Fold a second sheet into a fan in front of them and lay it across the same gap. Add pebbles one by one with the class counting aloud. It holds! Ask: "It is the same paper. What changed?" (The shape. It was folded.) Do not show the tube yet.
10 to 14 minExplain the challengeSay: "Today you are paper engineers. You will test 3 shapes: flat, fan and tube." Roll a tube and tape it. Show how to place pebbles gently and when to stop. Ask each pair to point to the shape they predict will win. Draw the recording layout (below) on the board.
14 to 30 minTest in pairsHelpers hand out 2 books, about 15 pebbles, 4 sheets and a piece of tape per pair. One partner places pebbles while the other counts, then they swap for the next shape. Roam with the questions below.
30 to 38 minRecordGive each student 1 sheet. They fold it in half, draw the 3 shapes on the front, write how many pebbles each held and circle the strongest. Helpers hand out journals. Students glue the sheet in and write the date and the title Paper Power.
38 to 45 minShare and pack up3 or 4 pairs share using the sentence stem on the board. Read the Success Criteria again and ask for thumbs. Finish with the key idea (below). Pack up: test paper into recycling, pebbles counted back into their container, books back, journals onto the shelves by the door.

Questions to ask while roaming

  • What happened when you added more pebbles?
  • Is it the same paper? So what changed?
  • Why do you think the fan held more than the flat paper?
  • Which shape do you think will win next? Why?

Support and extension

WhoWhat to do
SupportFold the first fan together with the pair. Students can record with drawings and numbers only. Pair a confident counter with a student who needs help counting.
ExtensionFast finishers (often Year 2) try a different fold, or a fat tube against a thin tube. Can they make a shape that holds even more? Write one sentence explaining why.
Key idea to finish on: Same paper, different shape, different strength. Folding and rolling made the paper stronger without adding anything new. Next week we will find out what happens when we twist paper together.

Notes for the teacher

  • Any result is fine. Most pairs find the flat sheet holds almost nothing and the fan holds the most. The comparison is the learning, not the exact numbers.
  • If a fan collapses straight away, check that the folds run from book to book.
  • Pebbles stay on the table and are placed, not dropped. Count them back into their container at pack up.
  • Nothing needs to be kept for next lesson.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
