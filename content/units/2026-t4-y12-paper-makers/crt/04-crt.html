Lesson 4: Mix It

Year 1/2 STEAM | Unit: Stronger Together: Paper Makers | CRT plan | 45 minutes

A little preparation (5 minutes). Check the class medical list for wheat or coeliac allergies. If there are any, use cornflour for the whole class. Cover each table with newspaper. Everything else is handed out by student helpers during the lesson. No screens are used.

This week each pair makes a layered paper strip that dries on the class tray for next week, and each student records a stick test in their STEAM journal. Journals are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To find out that mixing flour and water makes paste, a new material with different properties.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • describe what flour and water are like on their own.
  • mix them to make paste.
  • test which one sticks paper best.

Read these aloud at the start and come back to them at the end.

How to make paste and test it

  • Describe first: each student feels a pinch of flour between dry fingers (powdery, dry, soft) and looks at the water (runny, wet, it flows).
  • Make paste: 2 spoons of flour into the mixing cup, then 2 spoons of water. Stir 20 times. Too thick? Add a few drops of water. Too runny? Add a pinch of flour.
  • Make test squares: fold a sheet into quarters and tear on the folds to make 4 squares. Tear a long strip from the second sheet.
  • Stick test: stick 3 squares onto the strip: one with a pinch of dry flour, one with a little water and one with paste. Press each for a count of 5. Lift the strip so the squares hang underneath and give it a gentle shake.
  • Layer it: tear 3 more strips. Paste on the first, lay the second on top, paste again, lay the third on top. Write the pair's initials on the end in pencil and put it on the tray to dry.

Key words

powdery, runny, mix, stir, paste, sticky, new material, layer

Questions to ask while roaming

  • What does the flour feel like? What does the water look like?
  • What happened to the flour and water when you stirred them?
  • Is paste like flour? Is it like water? How is it different?
  • Which square stayed on? Why do you think it held?

Support and extension

WhoWhat to do
SupportSpoon the flour and water into the cup together, counting the stirs aloud. Students can record with ticks, crosses and drawings only.
ExtensionFast finishers (often Year 2) make a thick paste and a runny paste. Which one sticks better? Write one sentence explaining why.
Key idea to finish on: Flour on its own is powdery and water on its own is runny. Neither one sticks paper well. When we mix them, they make paste: a new material with new properties. It is sticky! Next week we will test the layered strips to see what the paste did to the paper.

Notes for the teacher

  • Allergies: if anyone has a wheat or coeliac allergy, use cornflour for the whole class. It makes a paste that works the same way. If there is no cornflour, that student records the stick test for their partner and does not touch the flour.
  • Raw flour is not safe to eat. Remind students not to taste the flour or the paste, and to keep their hands away from their mouths and eyes.
  • A wet water square can cling for a moment. That is fine: it falls off once it dries, and the paste square stays stuck.
  • Paste down the sink can block it. Scrape leftovers into the bin and rinse the cups.
  • The layered strips need a flat spot to dry overnight. Please do not throw them away: next lesson starts by testing them.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
