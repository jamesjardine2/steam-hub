Some preparation needed: check that the layered strips from Lesson 4 are dry and still on the class tray (if any are still damp, give them to the pair with a note and test them last). Tear or cut a single strip of the same paper for each pair, so the strips are about the same length and width. Set out two books per pair. All recorded work is glued into STEAM journals, which are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

## How to test the strips and design the carrier

- **Predict first:** each pair says which strip will hold more blocks, the single strip or the layered strip. Thumbs up or down.
- **Make the gap:** stand two books about one hand apart (10 cm). Lay a strip across the gap so each end rests on a book.
- **Add blocks:** place blocks one at a time in the middle of the strip, counting aloud. Stop when the strip sags and touches the table, or falls through. Write the number down.
- **Test the other strip:** do the same with the second strip. Compare the two numbers.
- **Choose the toy:** each pair picks a toy and measures it with a ruler or with hand spans. The carrier has to be big enough to hold it.
- **Draw the carrier:** draw it from the side with a base, sides and a handle. Use pencil first.
- **Label the techniques:** use at least two from the unit: folding, twisting, weaving or pasting. Draw an arrow to the part and write the technique, for example "woven sides" or "twisted handle".

**Key words:** design, carrier, technique, layer, stronger, handle, base

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle | Students sit on the floor facing you. Ask: "Last week, what did we make by mixing flour and water?" (Paste, a new material.) "What did we do with the paste?" (Stuck strips together in layers.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 8 min | CIC hook | Hold up a single strip and a dried layered strip. Ask: "Which will hold more blocks?" Thumbs up for single, thumbs down for layered. Set up the gap with two books and test the single strip in front of the class, counting blocks aloud until it sags. Then say: "Now the layered strip. Is the paste doing something?" Test it and compare the numbers. Ask: "What changed?" |
| 8 to 18 min | Test in pairs | Helpers hand out a layered strip (the pair's own, marked with their initials), a single strip, two books and a cup of blocks per pair. Pairs predict, test both strips and write both counts on the board table in their journals. Roam with the questions below. |
| 18 to 23 min | Model the design | Show the word wall for **design**, **carrier** and **technique**. Hold up a toy. Say: "Our job is to make a carrier that will hold this toy. It must be strong." On the board, draw a carrier from the side: a base, two sides and a handle. Add labels with arrows (for example "woven sides, because weaving was strong" and "pasted base"). Say aloud why each technique was chosen. |
| 23 to 38 min | Design in pairs | Pairs choose their toy and measure it. They draw their carrier with a base, sides and a handle and label at least two techniques, using pencil first. Ask pairs to write one sentence about why they chose each technique. Roam, then ask for a pair photo in the class Book Creator book if time allows. |
| 38 to 45 min | Share | 3 or 4 pairs share using the sentence stem on the board. Finish with the key idea (below). Journals onto the shelves by the door. |

## Draw this on the board

| | Single strip | Layered strip |
|---|---|---|
| How many blocks did it hold? | ______ | ______ |
| Which was stronger? | | |

Carrier plan labels: **base**, **sides**, **handle**, and an arrow to each technique (fold, twist, weave, paste).

Sentence stem: **Our carrier will be strong because we used ______ for the ______.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Which strip do you think will hold more? Why?
- How many blocks did the single strip hold? What about the layered strip?
- What is the paste doing to the paper?
- Where will your carrier carry the weight? Which technique will help there?
- How will you make your handle strong?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Give pairs a design sheet with a base, sides and handle outlined, so they only add the labels. Let students record with a drawing and a number instead of a sentence.
- **Extension:** Fast finishers (often Year 2) add measurements to their plan (for example "the base is 3 hand spans long") and draw a second handle or side idea. They write which idea is stronger and why.

**Key idea to finish on:** Layered paper held more blocks than a single strip, because the paste joined the layers so they work together. A design is a plan for something we will make. Next week we will make our carriers from our plans, so a good label now makes the making easier.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Dry strips:** the Lesson 4 layered strips need to be dry. If some are not, the test is still useful: a damp strip is weaker, so have those pairs record that and retest in Lesson 6.
- **Fair test:** the single strip and the layered strip should be the same paper, the same length and rest on the same gap. Check the gap with a ruler.
- **Falling blocks:** blocks can spill when a strip folds. Test over a table, with the books near the middle, and keep the cups of blocks away from the edge.
- **Counts vary:** results will differ between pairs, which is fine. Look for the pattern across the room, that most layered strips held more.
- **Toys:** use a small class toy, or a picture of one. Do not ask students to bring in a toy from home. Keep names and personal details out of the journals and the Book Creator book.
- **Lesson 6 links:** the design is the pair's plan for making the carrier next week. Collect or photograph the plans so they are ready for the Lesson 6 step card.
