No preparation needed. Everything is collected with student helpers during the lesson. No screens are used. This week each student's woven mat is glued straight into their STEAM journal. Journals are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

## How to weave and test

- **Make the loom:** fold one A4 sheet in half (short edge to short edge). Starting from the fold, cut 5 slits, stopping **a finger-width from the edge**. Open it out. The uncut border holds the loom together.
- **Make strips:** fold the second sheet in half the long way, then in half again. Open it and tear along the 3 folds to make 4 long strips. Tear one more sheet between two students if they need extra.
- **Weave:** push the first strip **over, under, over, under** through the slits. Push the next strip **under, over, under, over** (the opposite). Slide each strip up close to the last one. 4 or 5 strips fill the loom.
- **Carry test:** first lay 4 **loose** strips side by side and put pebbles in the middle. Lift by two edges. The strips slide apart and the pebbles fall. Then do the same with the **woven mat**: lift it by two edges like a hammock, just above the table, adding 1 pebble at a time. Count how many it carries.

**Key words:** weave, over, under, strip, loom, pattern, strong, carry

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle | Students sit on the floor facing you. Ask: "Last week, how did we make paper stronger?" (By twisting strips into a rope.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 9 min | CIC hook | Lay 4 loose paper strips side by side on the floor and put 3 pebbles on them. Ask: "If I lift these strips, will they carry the pebbles?" Thumbs up or down. Lift them by two edges. The strips slide apart and the pebbles fall! Ask: "How could we make these strips stay together without glue or tape?" Take ideas. Then ask students to look closely at the sleeve of their jumper or shirt: tiny threads are joined together to make fabric. Explain: people have been **weaving** for thousands of years. Aboriginal peoples, including the Wurundjeri People, weave baskets and bags from grass fibres to carry food and other things. Today we will weave with paper. |
| 9 to 14 min | Show how | Model the loom: fold, cut 5 slits from the fold, stop a finger-width from the edge, open. Model tearing strips. Model weaving the first strip (over, under) and the second strip (under, over), saying the pattern aloud with the class. |
| 14 to 31 min | Make and weave | Helpers hand out 2 sheets and scissors to each student. Students make their own loom and strips, then weave. As students finish, pairs collect about 15 pebbles and do the carry test: loose strips first, then the woven mat. Roam with the questions below. |
| 31 to 39 min | Record | Helpers hand out journals. Students glue their woven mat into their journal and write the date and the title **Weave It**. Underneath they copy and finish the sentences from the board. |
| 39 to 45 min | Share and pack up | 3 or 4 students share using the sentence stem. Finish with the key idea (below). Pack up: scraps into recycling, pebbles counted back into their container, scissors back, journals onto the shelves by the door. |

## Write this on the board

- Loose strips carried ___ pebbles.
- My woven mat carried ___ pebbles.

Sentence stem: **My woven mat is stronger than loose strips because ______.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What happened to the loose strips when you lifted them?
- What is holding your woven strips together? There is no glue!
- Is your woven mat made of anything new, or just paper?
- Where have you seen weaving before? (baskets, clothes, hats, mats)

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Cut the slits for students who find scissors tricky, or let them cut just 3 slits. Weave the first strip together, saying "over, under" aloud. Two woven strips is a success.
- **Extension:** Fast finishers (often Year 2) make a colour pattern (for example 2 colours, AB AB), weave the strips tighter, or fold the strip ends neatly onto the back. Can a tighter weave carry more pebbles? Write one sentence explaining why.

**Key idea to finish on:** Loose strips fall apart. When we weave them over and under, they hold each other in place and become one strong mat that can carry things. Same paper, combined in a new way, just like twisting last week. Next week we will mix two materials to make something new.

## Notes for the teacher

- The most common mistake is cutting slits right to the edge, which cuts the loom apart. Show "stop a finger-width from the edge" clearly. If a loom falls apart, tape the border back together on the back.
- If strips keep sliding out, have students push them close together, or tape the strip ends on the back.
- Woven mats do not have to be finished to be glued in. A half-woven mat still shows the learning.
- Lifting happens over the table, just above the surface, so pebbles do not fall far.
- Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
