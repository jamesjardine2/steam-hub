Some preparation needed: before the lesson, check the class medical list for wheat allergies (use cornflour for the whole class if needed), put about 3 heaped tablespoons of flour into a small cup for each pair, and cover the tables with newspaper. All recorded work is glued into STEAM journals, which are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

## How to make paste and test it

- **Describe first:** each student feels a pinch of flour between dry fingers (powdery, dry, soft) and looks at the water (runny, wet, it flows).
- **Make paste:** put 2 spoons of flour into the mixing cup, then 2 spoons of water. Stir 20 times. Too thick? Add a few drops of water. Too runny? Add a pinch of flour.
- **Make test squares:** fold a sheet of paper into quarters and tear on the folds to make 4 squares. Tear a strip from the second sheet as the test strip.
- **Stick test:** stick 3 squares onto the strip: one with a pinch of dry flour, one with a little water and one with paste. Press each one for a count of 5. Then lift the strip so the squares hang underneath, and give it a gentle shake.
- **Layer it:** tear 3 more strips. Spread paste on the first strip, lay the second strip on top, spread paste again, lay the third strip on top. Write the pair's initials in pencil on the end and put it on the class tray to dry for next week.

**Key words:** powdery, runny, mix, stir, paste, sticky, new material, layer

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle | Students sit on the floor facing you. Ask: "Last week, how did we make paper stronger?" (By weaving strips over and under.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 10 min | CIC hook | Hold up two small squares of paper. Ask: "Could flour stick these together?" Thumbs up or down. Sprinkle dry flour on one, press the other on top and lift: it falls off. Ask: "What about water?" Try again with a few drops of water: it slips off. Say: "Flour can't do it. Water can't do it... or can they? Watch." Mix 2 spoons of flour and 2 spoons of water in front of the class while they count the stirs to 20. Stick the squares with the paste and lift. It holds! Ask: "What changed?" |
| 10 to 15 min | Describe and model | Pass a cup of flour around for each student to feel a pinch. Build two word lists on the board: flour is ___ (powdery, dry, soft, white), water is ___ (runny, wet, it flows). Show the word wall for **mix** and **paste**. Model the stick test and the layered strip once, saying each step aloud. |
| 15 to 30 min | Make and test in pairs | Helpers hand out a flour cup, a water cup, 2 spoons, a mixing cup and 2 sheets of paper per pair. Pairs make paste, do the stick test with flour, water and paste, then make one layered strip with 3 strips and put it on the class tray. Roam with the questions below. |
| 30 to 39 min | Clean up and record | Pack up first: spoons and cups to the sink, leftover paste scraped into the bin, newspaper folded up, hands wiped. Give each student 1 sheet. They fold it in half and draw the 3 squares on the front, then tick the ones that stuck and cross the ones that fell. Helpers hand out journals. Students glue the folded sheet in and write the date and the title **Mix It**. Pairs photograph their stick test in the class Book Creator book with a smiley rating. |
| 39 to 45 min | Share | 3 or 4 pairs share using the sentence stem on the board. Finish with the key idea (below). Journals onto the shelves by the door. |

Sentence stem: **Paste is a new material because ______.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What does the flour feel like? What does the water look like?
- What happened to the flour and water when you stirred them?
- Is paste like flour? Is it like water? How is it different?
- Which square stayed on? Why do you think it held?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Spoon the flour and water into the cup together, counting the stirs aloud. Let students record with ticks, crosses and drawings only.
- **Extension:** Fast finishers (often Year 2) make a thick paste and a runny paste. Which one sticks better? Write one sentence explaining why.

**Key idea to finish on:** Flour on its own is powdery and water on its own is runny. Neither one sticks paper well. When we mix them, they make paste: a new material with new properties. It is sticky! Next week we will test the layered strips to see what the paste did to the paper.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Allergies:** check the medical list before the lesson. If anyone has a wheat or coeliac allergy, use cornflour for the whole class. Cornflour and water makes a paste that works the same way.
- **Food safety:** raw flour is not safe to eat. Remind students not to taste the flour or the paste, and to keep their hands away from their mouths and eyes.
- **Water may hold for a moment.** A wet paper square can cling while it is wet. If a pair's water square stays on, that is fine: leave one of each on the tray and check next week. The water square will fall off once it dries, and the paste square will still be stuck.
- **Paste down the sink** can block it. Scrape leftovers into the bin and rinse the cups.
- The layered strips need a flat spot to dry overnight. Lesson 5 opens by testing them against a single sheet.
- The unit overview had pairs pasting layers onto a card strip. This plan uses 3 paper strips instead, so the Lesson 5 test compares layered paper with a single sheet of the same paper.
