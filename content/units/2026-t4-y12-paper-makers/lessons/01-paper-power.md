No preparation needed. Everything is collected with student helpers during the lesson. No screens are used. All recorded work is glued into STEAM journals, which are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

## How the test works

- Place two books flat on the table, **one hand-span apart**. Keep this gap the same for every test so it is fair.
- Lay the paper across the gap like a bridge.
- Gently place pebbles **one at a time** in the middle. Count each one.
- Stop when the paper sags down to the table or collapses. That number is the score.
- Test 3 shapes: **Flat** (just lay it down), **Fan** (fold back and forth like a paper fan) and **Tube** (roll it up and tape it closed).
- For the fan, the folds must run **from book to book**, not across the gap.
- Pebbles can roll off a tube. If they do, place them in a small pile on top or count the pebble that falls as the last one.

**Key words:** strong, weak, shape, fold, roll, test, predict, weight

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle | Students sit on the floor facing you. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 10 min | CIC hook | Hold up one sheet of paper. Ask: "Could this piece of paper hold up these pebbles?" Thumbs up or down. Lay it flat across a gap between two books and place one pebble in the middle. It sags. Say: "Paper is weak... or is it? Watch." Fold a second sheet into a fan in front of them and lay it across the same gap. Add pebbles one by one with the class counting aloud. It holds! Ask: "It is the same paper. What changed?" Do not show the tube yet. |
| 10 to 14 min | Explain the challenge | Tell them: "Today you are paper engineers. You will test 3 shapes: flat, fan and tube." Show how to roll a tube and tape it. Show how to place pebbles gently one at a time and when to stop. Each pair points to the shape they predict will win. Draw the recording layout (below) on the board. |
| 14 to 30 min | Test in pairs | Choose helpers to hand out: 2 books, about 15 pebbles, 3 sheets and a piece of tape per pair. One partner places pebbles while the other counts, then swap for the next shape. Roam with the questions below. |
| 30 to 38 min | Record | Give each student 1 sheet. They fold it in half and draw the 3 shapes on the front, write how many pebbles each held, and circle the strongest. Helpers hand out journals. Students glue the folded sheet into their journal and write the date and the title **Paper Power**. |
| 38 to 45 min | Share and pack up | 3 or 4 pairs share using the sentence stem on the board. Finish with the key idea (below). Pack up: test paper into recycling, pebbles counted back into their container, books back, journals onto the shelves by the door. |

## Draw this on the board

| Flat | Fan | Tube |
|---|---|---|
| (draw it) | (draw it) | (draw it) |
| ___ pebbles | ___ pebbles | ___ pebbles |

Circle the strongest.

Sentence stem: **The ______ was strongest because ______.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What happened when you added more pebbles?
- Is it the same paper? So what changed?
- Why do you think the fan held more than the flat paper?
- Which shape do you think will win next? Why?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Fold the first fan together with the pair. Let them record with drawings and numbers only. Pair a confident counter with a student who needs help counting.
- **Extension:** Fast finishers (often Year 2) try a different fold, or a fat tube against a thin tube. Can they make a shape that holds even more? Write one sentence explaining why.

**Key idea to finish on:** Same paper, different shape, different strength. Folding and rolling made the paper stronger without adding anything new. Next week we will find out what happens when we twist paper together.

## Notes for the teacher

- Any result is fine. Most pairs find the flat sheet holds almost nothing and the fan holds the most. The comparison is the learning, not the exact numbers.
- If a fan collapses straight away, check that the folds run from book to book.
- Pebbles stay on the table and are placed, not dropped. Count them back into their container at pack up.
- Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any pairs who were absent.
