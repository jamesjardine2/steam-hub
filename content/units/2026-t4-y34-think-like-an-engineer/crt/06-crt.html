Lesson 6: LEGO Overhang

Year 3/4 STEAM | Unit: Think Like an Engineer: Design Challenge Series | CRT plan | 45 minutes

No preparation needed. Student helpers put out the LEGO tubs and rulers during the lesson. No screens are used.

Students build a LEGO arm that reaches out past the edge of the table, on their own or in a pair. There is no writing and no STEAM journals today: students say their predictions and results aloud, and only you write, on the board.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To build a LEGO arm that reaches past the edge of the table and use what happens when it falls to improve it.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • predict how far my arm will reach before I test it.
  • measure the reach with a ruler and say my result.
  • explain one change I made after a fall and why it helped.

Read these aloud at the start and come back to them at the end.

How the challenge works

  • The problem: build an arm that reaches as far past the edge of the table as it can.
  • Open design: any LEGO from the table tub, as many pieces as you need, any shape. The design is up to the students.
  • The constraints: LEGO only, built on the table. The arm stays up on its own for a count of 10. No hands, books, pencil cases or tape holding it down, and nothing under the arm.
  • Plan (1 minute): hands off the bricks. Talk through the idea and point to your prediction on the ruler: how many centimetres will it reach?
  • Build (4 minutes): build on the table, then slide the arm out over the edge.
  • Test: hands away, and the table counts to 10 together. If it stays up, measure along the top of the table from the edge to the tip, to the nearest centimetre. If it falls, it does not count: the result is 0, or the last reach that stayed up.
  • Three rounds: after each round, look at where it tipped and change one thing.

Use a watch or the classroom clock for the 1 minute and 4 minutes. Call "Hands off" at the end of each.

Key words

overhang (the part that sticks out past the edge), counterweight (weight at the back that stops it tipping), tipping point (the moment it falls), balance, reach, constraint

Lesson sequence

TimeStepWhat to do and say
0 to 3 minSettleStudents sit on the floor facing you. Ask: "Last week you tested paper helicopters. What was your variable, the one thing you changed?" (Wing length or paperclips.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud.
3 to 8 minHookLay a 30 cm ruler flat on a table and slide it slowly out over the edge. Ask: "Thumbs up when you think it will tip." It tips at about 15 cm, halfway. Now put a pencil case on the end that is still on the table and slide the ruler out again. It reaches much further. Ask: "Same ruler. Why does it reach further now?" Take two or three answers. (The pencil case holds the back down.) Do not explain any more yet.
8 to 15 minExplain and show howWrite overhang, counterweight and tipping point on the board. Say each word, the class repeats it, then say what it means. Read the challenge and the constraints (above). Build an arm in front of the class and think aloud: "I'll make a long straight arm and slide it out... it tips. Where did it tip? At the edge. So I need more weight on the table side. I'll move some bricks to the back as a counterweight." Slide it out, hands off, and count to 10 with the class. Measure from the edge to the tip. Show how to point to a prediction on the ruler and say the result to the table. Helpers hand out tubs and rulers.
15 to 31 minRounds 1 and 2Students choose to work alone or in a pair and take what they need from the tub, sharing fairly. Write the longest reach from each table on the board. Talk (2 minutes): "Where did your arm tip? What will you change?" Turn and talk, then two or three students answer with the sentence stem. Round 2: the same again. Ask: "Stand up if your arm reached further than in round 1."

(Most of the bricks stay on the table at the back, the part sticking out is long and light, and it is built out a little at a time.) The class says together: "Heavy at the back, light at the front." Questions to ask while roaming

  • What is your prediction? Why that number?
  • Where did your arm tip? What does that tell you?
  • Where is most of your weight: on the table or past the edge?
  • What will you change for the next round? Why?
  • Did the change help? How do you know?

Support and extension

WhoWhat to do
SupportWork in a pair and start from your model arm: a long arm with a stack of bricks on the back end. Measure the reach only, with no prediction.
ExtensionSet your own goal and test it: reach as far with fewer bricks, hold a rubber on the very tip for a count of 10, or reach out from two sides of a table corner at once.
Key idea to finish on: Engineers learn from what falls. Every fall shows where the design is weak. The arm stays up while most of its weight is over the table, so a counterweight at the back lets it reach further.

Notes for the teacher

  • Common mistakes: building the whole arm past the edge with nothing at the back; holding it down with a hand while counting; building up tall instead of out.
  • Fair measuring: hold the ruler flat on the table top, from the edge to the furthest point of the arm. Arms that do not last the count of 10 do not count.
  • Safety: falling LEGO drops to the floor. Keep the floor under the table edges clear and pick bricks up straight away so nobody slips or steps on them.
  • Pack up: all bricks go back in the tubs. Nothing needs to be kept.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
