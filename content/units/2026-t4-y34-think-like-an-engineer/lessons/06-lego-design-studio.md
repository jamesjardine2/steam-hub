**Setup:** none beyond the LEGO tubs on the tables. Put the base plates, marbles, scrap paper and the test book on a front table.

**Focus:** engineers design for a user. A design is good when it does what its user needs, and the way to find out is to test it against the criteria and let someone use it. The habit this week is **design for a user**.

**Links:** in Lessons 4 and 5 students tested how far, how many or how long. This lesson judges a design against criteria instead. It sets up Lesson 7, where groups prototype early and test often.

## The three challenges

Students choose one and work on their own or in a pair. Any LEGO from the table tub, any design.

**A. iPad stand.** For someone watching a video at their desk.
- It holds the iPad up for a count of 10.
- The user can see the screen from their chair.
- It stays up when the table is bumped gently.

**B. Desk organiser.** For a student whose pencils are always lost.
- It holds 4 pencils, a rubber and a ruler from the user's pencil case.
- Each thing has its own place.
- The user can take out each thing with one hand.

**C. Marble maze.** For a friend to solve.
- It has a start and a finish.
- The marble cannot escape when the maze is tilted.
- It has at least three turns.

**Key words:** user, design criteria, product, improve

## Brainstorm, sketch, build

- **Brainstorm (3 minutes):** talk only, hands off the bricks. Share as many ideas as you can with your partner or table. "What could it look like? What do other stands or organisers do?"
- **Sketch (4 minutes):** a quick drawing of the idea you will build first. It does not need to be neat, and it is not kept.
- **Build and improve (18 minutes):** build from the sketch, then test it against each criterion. When it is ready, ask a classmate to try it and show thumbs for each criterion. Fix the weakest part first, then test again.

Stands are tested with the hardcover book first. The iPad goes on only after the book stays up, and the teacher holds a hand just in front of it.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and recall | Students sit facing you. "In the helicopter test, what did you change? What stayed the same?" (Wing length or paperclips. The paper and the drop height.) |
| 3 to 7 min | Hook (CIC) | Lean an iPad against a pencil case on a table and let it slide down. "Thumbs: is this a good stand?" "Who would use a stand like this? What does it need to do?" Take two or three answers. (Hold the iPad up. Not slide. Let you see the screen.) "Those are design criteria." |
| 7 to 13 min | Explain and choose | Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria. Teach **user** (the person the design is for) and **design criteria** (what the design must do for the user): my turn, your turn. Show each challenge and read its three criteria. "Think... show me: 1, 2 or 3 fingers for the challenge you choose." Students choose on their own or in a pair. |
| 13 to 16 min | Brainstorm | Start the timer (7 minutes to plan, then 18 to build). Talk only, hands off the bricks. Roam and listen; ask "Who is it for? What does it need?" |
| 16 to 20 min | Sketch | Hand out scrap paper. "A quick sketch of the idea you will build first." |
| 20 to 38 min | Build and improve | When the timer turns to build, collect base plates and marbles from the front table. Roam with the questions below. At about 29 minutes, the halfway check: "Hands off. Test your design. Thumbs for each criterion." Then: "Fix your weakest part first. Ask a classmate to try it." |
| 38 to 42 min | Share | "Stand up if your design meets all three criteria." Two or three students show their design and share with the sentence stem. Say the key idea together. |
| 42 to 45 min | Pack up | Builds taken apart, bricks back in the tubs, marbles and base plates counted back to the front table, sketches into the recycling. |

Sentence stem: **My ___ is for ___. It meets ___. I improved ___ because ___.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Who is your design for? What do they need?
- Which criterion are you working on right now?
- How do you know it works? Show me.
- What did your classmate find? What will you change first?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** start with the desk organiser and a pair. Build a place for one thing at a time, testing each with the pencil case as you go.
- **Extension:** add a fourth criterion of your own and test it, for example "It holds the iPad sideways and upright" or "The maze has a trap the marble must avoid".

**Key idea to finish on:** engineers design for a user. A design is good when it meets the user's criteria, and the best way to find out is to test it and let someone use it.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Choices:** if one challenge is crowded, ask the next few to choose another, or limit the mazes to the base plates you have.
- **Marbles:** marbles catch on the studs. Build the path from smooth flat tiles, or swap to a ping pong ball. Count marbles out and back in.
- **iPad safety:** stands are tested with the hardcover book first. Only the teacher puts the iPad on a stand, low over the table, with a hand in front.
- **Common mistakes:** building before choosing who the design is for; skipping the sketch and changing plans every minute; testing only one criterion.
