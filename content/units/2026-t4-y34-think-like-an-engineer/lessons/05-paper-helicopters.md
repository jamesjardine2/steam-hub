**Setup:** before the lesson, cut the paper into strips (four per group, plus spares) and mark one drop height on the wall that every group uses, for example a line a student can reach with a straight arm. Put scissors, paperclips and a timer on each table. Groups of three work well: a dropper, a timer and a recorder, swapping jobs for the second helicopter.

**Focus:** engineers change one thing at a time so they know what caused the difference. The habit this week is **change one thing at a time**.

**Links:** in Lesson 4 students ran a fair test with foil boats. This lesson adds a name for the thing you change (the variable) and repeats each test three times. It also starts the Challenge Card, which every lesson to Lesson 7 uses (Lesson 4 had no writing).

## How the challenge works

- **The problem:** which change makes a paper helicopter fall the slowest?
- **The constraints:** one paper strip per helicopter, one paperclip at most unless paperclips are the variable, and every drop starts from the marked height.
- **Choose one variable:** each group picks one thing to change: wing length, or the number of paperclips. Everything else stays the same.
- **Predict:** the group writes which version they think will fall slower, and why.
- **Fair test:** drop each helicopter three times from the marked height. The timer records each fall. These are the trials.
- **Decide:** compare the times and say which change worked best. Write one change to try next.

**Key words:** variable, trial, control, fair test, prediction, evidence

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and recall | Students sit facing you. Ask: "In the boat test, what did we keep the same?" (The foil, the pebbles, the way we added them.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 8 min | Hook (CIC) | Hold up two paper helicopters that are the same except for their wings, one long and one short. Drop them together from the marked height. Ask: "Which one landed last? What made the difference?" Take two or three answers. Do not explain yet. |
| 8 to 16 min | Explain and model | Show the word wall: **variable** (the one thing you change in a test), **trial** (one go at the test; we do three) and **control** (what you keep the same). Play the how-to animation. Then think aloud: "My variable is wing length. I keep the paper, the paperclip and the drop height the same. I drop it three times and write each time down, because one go could be a fluke." This is the first Challenge Card, so show the class each part as you fill it in with your example: the challenge, the constraints, the sketch, the three numbered lines for prediction and result, and one change. |
| 16 to 25 min | Plan and build | Groups choose one variable and write a prediction on their Challenge Card. Then they build a standard helicopter and a second one that changes only the variable. Check each group can say, "Our variable is ___." |
| 25 to 40 min | Test and improve | Groups drop each helicopter three times from the marked height, time every fall and record it. Roam with the questions below. Groups that finish choose the best change and test it again, or test a second variable. |
| 40 to 45 min | Share and pack up | Pack up first: strips into the recycling and scissors back to the tubs. Then 3 or 4 groups share their variable, their times and the best change, using the sentence stem. Say the key idea together. Challenge Cards glued into STEAM journals. |

Sentence stem: **Our variable was ___. The ___ version fell slower, so next time we would ___ because ___.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What is your variable? What are you keeping the same?
- Why do we drop it three times, not just once?
- Are all three times close together? What might make one different?
- What does your evidence tell you? What would you change next?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** give a group the standard helicopter to copy and one variable to test (paperclips only). Let them record with tally marks or numbers only.
- **Extension:** test a second variable on the best version, or find the average of the three trials. Predict first, then test.

**Key idea to finish on:** engineers change one thing at a time and repeat the test, so the evidence tells them what made the difference.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Drop height:** a consistent drop is the hardest part of the fair test. A line on the wall and a straight arm works better than a chair.
- **Safety:** scissors are used for cutting only. Students stay behind the drop line and chairs stay on the floor.
- **Helicopter shape:** [Full folding plan to confirm: cut a slit down the centre of the top half, fold the two wings in opposite directions and add a paperclip to the base.]
- **Timing:** short falls are hard to time. If stopwatches are short, count claps together or use a taller mark.
- Keep one or two Challenge Cards as models for Lesson 6, which uses the same card.
