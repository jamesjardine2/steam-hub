**Setup:** before the lesson, cut the foil into squares of the same size (two per group, plus spares), put about 60 pebbles in a container for each tub, and set shallow tubs of water on towels, one tub for every two groups. Groups of three work well: a builder, a placer and a counter, swapping jobs for the second test.

**Focus:** engineers predict before they test, then test fairly so the result means something. The habit this week is **predict, then test**.

**Links:** in Lessons 1 to 3 students built and improved LEGO designs, and they built paper bridges that held pebbles. This lesson starts the weekly Challenge Card, which every lesson to Lesson 7 uses.

## How the challenge works

- **The problem:** build a boat that floats and holds as many pebbles as possible.
- **The constraints:** one square of foil per boat, no tape or other materials, and 6 minutes to build. Every group's boat is tested the same way.
- **Predict:** before building, the group sketches their boat on the Challenge Card and writes how many pebbles they think it will hold.
- **Fair test:** put the boat gently on the water. The placer adds pebbles one at a time in the middle of the boat while the counter counts aloud. The score is the last pebble before water comes in or the boat sinks.
- **Improve:** compare the result with the prediction, write one change on the card, and if there is time, build the change with a fresh square and test again.

**Key words:** constraint, prediction, fair test, float, sink, design, improve

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and recall | Students sit facing you. Ask: "When we built paper bridges, what made the same paper hold more pebbles?" (Changing its shape.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 8 min | Hook (CIC) | Hold up two squares of foil the same size. Fold one into a simple boat in front of the class and leave the other flat. Ask: "Same foil. Which one will hold more pebbles? How many?" Students show a number on their fingers. Put the flat square on the water and add pebbles one at a time while the class counts. Leave the boat untested: "That's your job today." |
| 8 to 15 min | Explain and model | Show the word wall: **constraint** (a rule or limit the design must follow), **prediction** (what you think will happen, and why) and **fair test** (only the boat changes; everything else stays the same). Read the constraints from the slide. Then think aloud through a fair test with the how-to animation: "I'll write my prediction first, so I can't change it later. I put the boat down gently. I add one pebble at a time, always in the middle, and my partner counts. My score is the last pebble before water comes in." Model filling in the Challenge Card with your flat-square result. |
| 15 to 20 min | Plan and predict | Groups get a Challenge Card. They sketch their boat from the side and write their prediction. Check every group has a prediction written before handing out foil. |
| 20 to 37 min | Build, test and improve | Hand out one foil square per group. Build for 6 minutes (use a timer), then groups take turns at the tubs: builder places the boat, placer adds pebbles, counter counts. Record the result on the card. Groups that finish write one change, build it with a fresh square and test again. Roam with the questions below. |
| 37 to 45 min | Share and pack up | Pack up first: pebbles back in containers, foil balled up for recycling, tubs emptied by an adult, towels hung up. Then 3 or 4 groups share their prediction, their result and one change, using the sentence stem. Say the key idea together. Challenge Cards glued into STEAM journals. |

Sentence stem: **We predicted ___ pebbles and our boat held ___. Next time we would ___ because ___.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What is your prediction? Why do you think that?
- Is your test fair? What are you keeping the same each time?
- Where did the water come in first? What could you change there?
- Was your result more or less than your prediction? Why do you think that happened?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** give a group a boat shape to copy (a flat base with the sides folded up), so the challenge is the test and the prediction. Let them record with a sketch and numbers only.
- **Extension:** test one change at a time. Does a wider base or higher sides make the bigger difference? Predict first, then test both with fresh squares.

**Key idea to finish on:** engineers predict before they test, and they test fairly so they can trust the result. Comparing the result with the prediction tells us what to change next.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Water safety:** keep the tubs on towels on the floor or a low table, and wipe spills straight away so the floor is not slippery. An adult empties the tubs.
- **Pebbles:** use pebbles of a similar size so results can be compared. Marbles work too, but roll off tables, so keep them in containers.
- **Foil edges** can be sharp. Fold them over if a student is worried.
- The flat square in the hook usually holds very few pebbles before water comes over the edge. That is the point: the material is the same, the shape is different, just like the paper bridge.
- Keep one or two Challenge Cards as models for Lesson 5, which uses the same card.
