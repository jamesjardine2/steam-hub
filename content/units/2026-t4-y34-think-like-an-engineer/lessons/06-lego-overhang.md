**Setup:** none. Student helpers put a tub of LEGO on each table and hand out rulers. Students choose to work on their own or in a pair, and take what they need from their table's tub, sharing fairly. Clear the floor under the table edges so falling bricks are easy to pick up.

**Focus:** engineers learn from what goes wrong. A fall is not a failure: it shows exactly where the design is weak. The habit this week is **learn from what falls**.

**No writing today:** predictions and results are said aloud or pointed to on the ruler. Only the teacher writes, on the board.

**Links:** in Lesson 5 students changed one thing at a time and repeated each test. This lesson keeps the three rounds and adds measuring with a ruler. Like Lesson 4, there is no writing. It also sets up Lesson 7, where groups prototype early and test often.

## How the challenge works

- **The problem:** build an arm that reaches as far past the edge of the table as it can.
- **Open design:** any LEGO from the table tub, as many pieces as you need, any shape: a straight arm, steps, a wide base, a long beam. The design is up to you.
- **The constraints:** LEGO only, built on the table. The arm must stay up on its own for a count of 10: no hands, books, pencil cases or tape holding it down. Nothing may sit under the arm.
- **Plan (1 minute):** hands off the bricks. Talk through the idea and point to your prediction on the ruler: how many centimetres will it reach?
- **Build (4 minutes):** build on the table and slide the arm out over the edge.
- **Test:** take your hands away and the table counts to 10 together. If it stays up, measure from the table edge to the tip with a ruler, to the nearest centimetre. If it falls, the reach is the last measurement that stayed up, or 0 in round 1.
- **Three rounds:** after each round, look at where it tipped and change one thing.

**Key words:** overhang, counterweight, tipping point, balance, reach, constraint

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and recall | Students sit facing you. Ask: "In the helicopter test, what was your variable? What did you keep the same?" (Wing length or paperclips. The paper and the drop height.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 8 min | Hook (CIC) | Lay a 30 cm ruler flat on a table and slide it slowly out over the edge. "Thumbs up when you think it will tip." It tips at about 15 cm, halfway. Now put a pencil case on the end that is still on the table and slide it out again, much further. "Same ruler. Why does it reach further now?" Take two or three answers. Do not explain yet. |
| 8 to 15 min | Explain and model | Write three words on the board and say each one, class repeats: **overhang** (the part that sticks out past the edge), **counterweight** (weight at the back that stops it tipping) and **tipping point** (the moment it falls). Read the challenge and the constraints. Think aloud as you build: "I'll make a long straight arm... it tips. Where did it tip? At the edge. So I need more weight on the table side. I'll move some bricks to the back as a counterweight." Slide it out, hands off, count to 10 with the class, then measure from the edge to the tip. Show how to point to a prediction on the ruler and say the result. |
| 15 to 22 min | Round 1 | Students choose solo or a pair and take what they need from the tub. 1 minute to plan: hands off, talk it through, point to a prediction on the ruler. 4 minutes to build. Then hands away, count to 10, measure and say the result to the table. Write the longest reach from each table on the board. |
| 22 to 24 min | Talk | "Where did your arm tip? What will you change?" Turn and talk, then two or three answers using the sentence stem. |
| 24 to 31 min | Round 2 | As round 1. "Stand up if your arm reached further than in round 1." |
| 31 to 34 min | What makes a long reach? | "Look at the longest reaches on the board. What is the same about them?" Turn and talk. Bring out: most of the bricks stay on the table at the back, the part that sticks out is long and light, and it is built out a little at a time. Class says together: "Heavy at the back, light at the front." |
| 34 to 41 min | Round 3 | As before, using what the class found out. |
| 41 to 45 min | Share and pack up | Pack up first: bricks back in the tubs, rulers collected. Then 3 or 4 students share their best reach and one change, using the sentence stem. Say the key idea together. |

Sentence stem: **My arm tipped when ___, so I changed ___ and it reached ___ cm.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- What is your prediction? Why that number?
- Where did your arm tip? What does that tell you?
- Where is most of your weight: on the table or past the edge?
- What will you change for the next round? Why?
- Did the change help? How do you know?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** work in a pair and start from the model arm (a long arm with a stack of bricks on the back end). Measure the reach only, with no prediction.
- **Extension:** set your own goal and test it: reach as far with fewer bricks, hold a rubber on the very tip for a count of 10, or reach out from two sides of the table corner at once.

**Key idea to finish on:** engineers learn from what falls. The arm stays up while most of its weight is over the table, so a counterweight at the back lets it reach further.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Fair measuring:** measure along the top of the table from the edge to the furthest point of the arm, holding the ruler flat. Arms that do not last the count of 10 do not count.
- **Common mistakes:** building the whole arm past the edge with nothing at the back; holding it down with a hand while counting; stacking tall instead of reaching out.
- **Safety:** falling LEGO drops to the floor. Keep the floor under the edge clear, pick bricks up straight away, and keep feet clear of loose bricks.
- **Pack up:** take every arm apart and return all bricks to the tubs. Nothing needs to be kept.
