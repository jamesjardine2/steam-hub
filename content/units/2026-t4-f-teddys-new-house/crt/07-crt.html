Lesson 7: The Three Little Pigs

Foundation STEAM | Unit: Teddy's New House | CRT plan | 45 minutes

No preparation needed. This is a stand-alone lesson. There is no building and no LEGO today. The story video is already set up on the TV with the iPad: just press play. If the video will not play, tell the story using the short version below.

Each student makes a three house fold-out book and sticks it into their STEAM journal. Journals are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To learn that some materials make a stronger house than others.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • retell the story of the three little pigs.
  • say which house was the strongest.
  • say why bricks make a strong house.

Read these aloud at the start and come back to them at the end.

How to make the fold-out book

  • Fold: turn the paper sideways. Fold it into three equal parts, like a letter, then open it flat.
  • Draw: the straw house in the first part, the stick house in the second and the brick house in the third.
  • Label: write fell down under the straw and stick houses and stayed up under the brick house. Students copy from the board or you write it for them.
  • Close it: fold the book shut and draw the wolf on the front.
  • Stick it in: put glue on the back of the brick house part only and press it into the journal, so the book still opens.

Key words

strong, weak, material, straw, sticks, bricks, heavy

The story, if the video will not play

Three little pigs left home to build their own houses. The first pig was in a hurry. He built his house from straw. The wolf came and knocked: "Little pig, little pig, let me come in!" "Not by the hair on my chinny chin chin!" So the wolf huffed and he puffed and he blew the house down. The pig ran to his brother's house.

The second pig built his house from sticks. The wolf huffed and he puffed and he blew that house down too. Both pigs ran to their sister's house.

The third pig took a long time. She built her house from bricks. The wolf huffed and he puffed and he huffed and he puffed, but the house did not move. Bricks are strong and heavy. The three pigs were safe inside.

Sentence stem: The brick house stayed up because ______.

Questions to ask while roaming

  • Which house are you drawing? What is it made of?
  • Why did the straw house fall down?
  • Why couldn't the wolf blow down the brick house?
  • Which house would you build for Teddy? Why?

Support and extension

WhoWhat to do
SupportFold the paper for the student. They draw the houses, and you write the labels while they tell you which house fell down.
ExtensionOn the back of the book, draw a fourth house for Teddy that the wolf could not blow down, and write or say what it is made of.
Key idea to finish on: The pigs used different materials. Straw and sticks are light, so the wolf blew them down. Bricks are strong and heavy, so the brick house stayed up. When we build a house for Teddy, we choose strong materials too.

Notes for the teacher

  • Folding into three is tricky for Foundation students. Show it slowly and help the first few, then early finishers can help their neighbours.
  • Gluing the whole sheet down stops the book opening. Remind students: glue on the back of the brick house part only.
  • Please do not use LEGO or building materials today. They are saved for next week.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
