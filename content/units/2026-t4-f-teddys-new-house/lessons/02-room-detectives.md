No preparation is needed. Everything is in the **labelled tub on the STEAM desk**: the handouts (one per student, plus one for you to hold up), the Design challenge cards already cut into sets, scissors and glue sticks. STEAM journals are in the class tub. Bring the class straight to the mat and start with the hook.

**Focus:** Rooms in a home are designed for different jobs, and the things inside each room help people do those jobs.

**Links:** Last lesson students sorted homes and not-homes. This lesson they go inside a home.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Hook (CIC) | **Which room am I in?** Tell students you are going to act something out and they have to guess which room of the house you are in. Mime each one slowly with no words: **brushing your teeth** (bathroom), **stirring a pot and tasting it** (kitchen), **yawning, fluffing a pillow and pulling up a blanket** (bedroom). After each guess, ask: "How did you know?" Then choose 2 or 3 students to mime one for the class. |
| 5 to 15 min | Talk about the pictures | Remind students: "Last week we found out every home keeps people safe, dry and comfy. Today we are going inside a home." Hold up the handout and point to each picture. Students call out which room it belongs in. Ask one quick question per picture, for example "What job does this do?" or "Why would we keep this in the kitchen and not the bedroom?" Then ask a tricky one with no picture: "Remember the tent and the caravan? They only have one or two rooms. Where do people cook, sleep and wash?" Let partners talk, then share. Finish with the big idea, said together: **"Every room has a job."** |
| 15 to 20 min | Model the journal | Show students how to set up their STEAM journal: open to a new double page and glue the 3 room labels **down the left side**, one at the top, one in the middle and one at the bottom. The pictures will go in a row next to their room. Model cutting out one picture, deciding which room it belongs in, and gluing it next to that label. While you model, choose one helper per table to put out scissors, glue sticks, journals and a set of Design challenge cards (face down). |
| 20 to 38 min | Cut, sort, stick | At tables, students write their name in their journal, then cut out the 3 labels and all 12 pictures. They **sort the pictures on the table first**, then glue them into their journal next to the correct room. Walk around and ask each student: "What is this used for? Why does it belong in this room?" **Early finishers:** choose a Design challenge card. Read it to them, then they draw the room on the next journal page and tell you about the things they put in it. Write a label for them if needed. |
| 38 to 45 min | Share and reflect | Choose 3 or 4 students to show their journal and explain why one thing belongs in its room. Read the Success Criteria aloud and students show a thumb (up, sideways or down) for each one. Pack away scissors and glue, and collect journals. |

## The handout

One A4 sheet per student with 3 room labels and 12 photos of things from around a home. The photos show each object on its own, so students have to think about where it belongs rather than spotting the room in the background.

| Room | Pictures |
|---|---|
| Kitchen | Fridge, stove with oven, kettle, toaster |
| Bedroom | Bed, wardrobe, folded pyjamas, bedside lamp |
| Bathroom | Toilet, bathtub, toothbrush and toothpaste, shower |

## Questions to ask

- What job does this room do?
- What would happen if the fridge was in the bedroom?
- Why is the toilet in its own room with a door?
- Which room do you like best at home? What do you do there?
- If you could add a new room to your home, what job would it do?

## What to look for

| Success criterion | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|
| Sorts things into rooms | All 12 pictures glued next to the correct room label in the STEAM journal. |
| Says what job a room does | Uses words like cook, eat, sleep, wash, get dressed when talking about a room. |
| Tells why something belongs | Links the object to the job, for example "The kettle goes in the kitchen because it makes hot water for cooking." |

## Design challenge cards

These are for students who finish the sort early and are ready to think like designers. Read the card aloud. A strong response is a room with things in it that match the person's needs.

| Card | A room for... | Look for things like |
|---|---|---|
| Baby | A new baby | A cot, a change table, soft toys, a night light |
| Pet | A pet dog | A dog bed, food and water bowls, toys, a little door to get outside |
| Wheelchair | Someone who uses a wheelchair | A wide door, a ramp, a low bed, shelves and benches they can reach |

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Pre-cut the pictures for students who find cutting hard. Give the sentence starter: "This goes in the ___ because ___."
- **Extension:** Design challenge cards (see above). Students who finish a card can choose a second one.

## Notes for the CRT

- **Sort before you stick.** Remind students to lay every picture on the table next to the right label before any glue comes out.
- **A dot, not a lot** of glue on the back of each picture.
- If time is short, skip the tent and caravan question.
- Please leave the **STEAM journals** in the class tub on the STEAM desk. A short note about how the class went is always appreciated.
