No preparation is needed. The **LEGO tubs and base plates** are on the table next to the STEAM desk. Teddy's letter is with this plan (page 5 of the PDF). STEAM journals are in the class tub. Bring the class straight to the mat and grab a handful of LEGO bricks for the demonstration.

**Focus:** The way we join materials together can make a house strong. Overlapping bricks makes a much stronger wall than stacking them in towers.

**Links:** Students have looked at homes from the outside (Lesson 1) and the inside (Lesson 2). Now they meet Teddy, who needs a new house. Teddy's house is the thread through Lessons 4 to 7.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Hook (CIC) | **A letter from Teddy.** Read Teddy's letter aloud with a sad voice. Ask: "What happened to Teddy's house? Why did it fall down?" Let a few students answer. Then: "Teddy needs our help. Today we are going to be builders and find out how to make walls that do not fall down." |
| 5 to 12 min | Wall test | On the mat, build two short walls in front of the class (see below). **Wall A:** stack bricks straight up in 3 separate towers, side by side. **Wall B:** overlap the bricks, like a real brick wall. Ask: "Which wall do you think is stronger?" Students point. Then gently push the top of each wall. Wall A breaks apart; Wall B holds together. Ask: "Why did Wall B stay together?" Finish with the big idea, said together: **"Overlap the bricks to make a strong wall."** If there is a brick wall visible from the room, point it out. |
| 12 to 14 min | Builder rules | Explain the job: "With your partner, build a house for Teddy on a base plate. It needs 4 walls with overlapping bricks and a door. If you finish, add a roof." Rules: share the bricks, build on the base plate only, and no breaking other people's houses. Put students in pairs and send each pair to a table. Helpers put out one base plate per pair and one LEGO tub per table. |
| 14 to 28 min | Build | Students build. Walk around and ask: "Show me where you overlapped your bricks. How tall is your wall? Count the bricks." If you see towers instead of overlapping, point to Wall B and ask: "Does your wall look like this one?" |
| 28 to 33 min | Wobble test | Stop the class. Tell them a big wind is coming. On your count, each pair holds their base plate **low over the table** and gently wobbles it while everyone counts to 5. Then: "Did anything fall off? Which part broke?" Give pairs 2 minutes to **fix the weak part**, then do the wobble test again. |
| 33 to 38 min | Journal | Each student draws their house in their STEAM journal and draws a circle around the strongest part. Walk around and write a short label for them in their words, for example "Strong because we overlapped." |
| 38 to 41 min | Share and reflect | Choose 2 or 3 pairs to show their house and say how they made it strong. Read the Success Criteria aloud and students show a thumb (up, sideways or down) for each one. Teaser: "Next week we will find out how to keep Teddy dry in the rain." |
| 41 to 45 min | Pack up | Houses are pulled apart and all bricks go back in the tubs. Base plates are stacked on the LEGO table. Journals go back in the class tub. |

## The wall test: what to build

Use the same size bricks for both walls, about 3 bricks wide and 5 bricks tall. In **Wall A** the bricks sit straight on top of each other, so each column is a separate tower (weak). In **Wall B** each brick sits across the join of the two bricks below it (strong). The PDF has a picture of both.

## Teddy's letter

> Dear Foundation,
>
> I need your help! I built my house, but when the big wind came, my walls wobbled and my house fell down. Now I have nowhere to live. Can you help me build a house with strong walls that will not fall down?
>
> Love from,
> Teddy

## Questions to ask

- Where did you overlap your bricks?
- How many bricks tall is your wall?
- What happened in the wobble test? Which part broke?
- How did you fix it? Is it stronger now?
- Real builders overlap bricks too. Can you think of a brick wall you have seen?

## What to look for

| Success criterion | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|
| Overlaps bricks | Walls show bricks sitting across joins, not separate towers. |
| Builds a house with a partner | House on the base plate has 4 walls and a door; partners share bricks and take turns. |
| Tests and fixes | After the wobble test, can point to the part that broke and show how they made it stronger. |

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Build the first row of the wall together with the pair, then let them continue. Give the sentence starter: "My house is strong because ___."
- **Extension:** Give these challenges out loud to pairs who finish early: **add a window**; **add a roof** that covers the whole house; **make it tall enough** for a LEGO person to stand inside; **build a second floor**. After each one, do another wobble test.

## Notes for the CRT

- **Keep the wobble gentle.** Base plates stay low over the table so nothing drops on the floor.
- **Leave 4 minutes** to pack up: houses are pulled apart and every brick goes back in the tubs, so nothing needs to be stored.
- Please leave the **STEAM journals** in the class tub on the STEAM desk. A short note about how the class went is always appreciated.
