**Setup:** Before the class arrives, make a pack for each group: one tray, four squares of material (paper, foil, plastic, cloth) cut to the same size, four paper towels, a small cup of water and a dropper or spoon. Keep spare paper towels nearby for spills. [Check the STEAM cupboard for droppers; a spoon works if there are none.]

**Focus:** Different materials are good for different jobs. A roof needs a material that keeps water out.

**Links:** In Lesson 3 students built strong walls for Teddy's house. Rain is coming, so now Teddy needs a roof. The material students choose here is used when they plan (Lesson 5) and build (Lesson 6) the house.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Hook (CIC) | **Rain is coming!** Show the slide of sad Teddy and the rain. Say: "Teddy's walls are strong, but the rain is coming and he has no roof." Hold up the four materials one at a time and name them. Ask: "Which one could keep Teddy dry?" |
| 5 to 10 min | Predict | Students point to the material they think will keep the rain out and tell a partner why. Take a few ideas. Introduce the key words with the word wall: a **roof** is the top of a house; **waterproof** means water cannot get through; to **soak** is to take water in. Read the Learning Intention and the Success Criteria. |
| 10 to 14 min | Demonstrate the test | Show the how-to slides. Model one test with one material: put the paper towel on the tray, put the material on top, drip 10 drops (children count aloud), lift the material, look at the towel. Ask: "Is the towel wet or dry? What does that tell us about the material?" Remind children of the safety rules (water stays on the tray, drip gently, wipe spills). |
| 14 to 28 min | Water test | Groups test all four materials, taking turns to drip. A fresh paper towel for each test. Walk around and ask: "What do you think will happen? Is the towel wet or dry? Did the water go through?" Use the words **waterproof** and **soak** as they come up. |
| 28 to 35 min | Share results | Bring the class to the mat. Ask: "Which materials let the water through? Which kept the towel dry?" Give the class a moment to think, then ask which material they would choose for Teddy's roof. Say together: **"A waterproof material keeps Teddy dry."** |
| 35 to 45 min | Journal and pack up | Each student draws Teddy's best roof in their STEAM journal and labels the material (write the label for them if needed). Then tip out cups, wipe trays, put used paper towels in the bin and return the materials. Teaser: "Next week we will plan Teddy's whole house." |

## What to draw on the board

Draw a four-column chart with the material names and a picture of each (paper, foil, plastic, cloth). After the test, ask students to come up and add a tick for "dry" or a cross for "wet" under each. Leave the chart up as the class result.

## Questions to ask

- Which material do you think will keep the water out? Why?
- Was the paper towel wet or dry? What happened to the water?
- Which materials soaked up the water?
- Which material would you choose for Teddy's roof?
- Where have you seen a roof made from that material?

## What to look for

| Success criterion | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|
| Predicts | Points to or names a material and gives a reason. |
| Tests | Drips water onto each material and looks at the towel underneath. |
| Chooses the best roof | Says which material kept the towel dry and uses the word waterproof. |

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Give the sentence starter: "The ___ kept Teddy dry because the water ___." Work with the group to do the first test together.
- **Extension:** Ask students to rank the four materials from best to worst roof. Or ask: "What if the rain is heavier? Add 20 drops. Does your answer change?"

## The key idea

A roof needs a waterproof material. We find out which material is waterproof by testing it. Students will use the best roof material when they plan and build Teddy's house.

## Notes for the CRT

- **Water stays on the tray.** Have spare paper towels ready and mop up spills straight away so the floor is not slippery.
- **Use a small amount of water** in each cup, and fill the cups before the class arrives.
- **Leave 10 minutes** for the journal and pack up. Tip the water down the sink, bin the wet paper towels and return the materials to the STEAM desk.
- Please leave the **STEAM journals** in the class tub on the STEAM desk. A short note about how the class went is always appreciated.
