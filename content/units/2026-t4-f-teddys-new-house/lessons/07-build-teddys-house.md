**Setup:** Cut a plastic roof sheet for each pair and stick tape strips along the edge of each table. Set out the class tray of card doors from Lesson 6, with a few spares. Have the STEAM journals open at the plans from Lesson 5.

**Focus:** Builders follow their plan, one part at a time, and check it as they go. Each material has a job: LEGO for strong walls, card and tape for a door that opens, plastic for a roof that keeps Teddy dry.

**Links:** This lesson brings the unit together: overlapping bricks from Lesson 3, the plastic roof from the Lesson 4 water test, the plan from Lesson 5 and the hinged door from Lesson 6. In Lesson 8 the houses are rebuilt from the plans, tested in the rain and shown on a house tour.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Recall | Quick questions, think time, then thumbs or answers: "How do we make a wall strong?" (Overlap the bricks.) "Which material kept Teddy dry?" (Plastic.) "Where does the tape go for a hinge?" (Down one side.) |
| 3 to 7 min | Hook | Hand back the journals. "Last time you planned Teddy's house. Today you build what your plan shows." Each pair looks at both plans and chooses one to build. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria. |
| 7 to 12 min | Word wall | Use the word wall. A **base** is the flat part a building sits on. A **material** is what something is made of. A **structure** is something built from parts that holds together. Students think first, then each box is uncovered. For each non-example, ask "Why is this not a ___?" and use the reasons below. |
| 12 to 17 min | I do | Build in front of the class, checking the plan before each step. "My plan says walls first. I start on the base. I overlap the bricks so the wall is strong. I leave a gap for the door and put a brick across the top. Now the door: tape down one side. Now the roof: I fold the plastic in half and sit it on the walls, then tape each end." |
| 17 to 37 min | You do | Pairs build one part at a time: base and walls (with a doorway), then stop for a teacher check (overlap, doorway with a brick across), then door, then roof. Roam with the questions below. |
| 37 to 41 min | Check the plan | Pairs hold the plan beside the house. Point to each part on the plan, then on the house: thumbs up if it matches. Anything they changed, they draw onto the plan, so they can build it again next week. |
| 41 to 45 min | Pack up | Photograph each house with its plan. Doors and plans go in the class tray, then the houses are pulled apart and bricks go back in the tubs. |

## Word wall: why each non-example is not the word

Say these after students have had a go at explaining.

- **Base.** A roof: it is at the top, not the bottom. A wall: it stands up, and nothing sits on it. A bumpy pile of bricks: it is not flat, so the house would wobble.
- **Material.** Red: it is what it looks like, not what it is made of. A square: it is a shape, not what it is made of. A door: it is a thing, and card is its material.
- **Structure.** A pile of bricks: the parts are there, but they do not hold together. One brick on its own: it is only one part. A drawing of a house: it is a plan, not something built.

## How the roof goes on

Fold the plastic sheet in half like a tent. Sit it on top of the two side walls so it covers the house, then press a strip of tape over each end where the plastic touches the bricks. [Check the plastic stays on when the house is moved; if not, a LEGO brick on each corner holds it down.]

## Questions to ask

- What does your plan say comes next?
- How do you know your wall is strong?
- Which material did you use for the roof? Why that one?
- Does your door open? Where is the hinge?
- Is your house the same as your plan? What is different?

## What to look for

| Success criterion | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|
| Builds strong walls on a base | Bricks overlap; the walls stand on a base plate without wobbling. |
| Adds a door and a plastic roof | The card door swings on a tape hinge; the plastic covers the top of the house. |
| Checks it matches the plan | Points to parts on the plan and the house; draws any changes onto the plan. |

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Build the first row of the wall with the pair, then they copy the overlap pattern for the next rows.
- **Extension:** Add a window that opens (a flap hinge from Lesson 6), and draw it onto the plan first.

## The key idea

Builders follow their plan, one part at a time. Each material has a job: LEGO for strong walls, tape for a hinge, plastic to keep Teddy dry.

## Notes for the CRT

- Cut the plastic roofs and tear the tape before the class arrives.
- Please photograph each house next to its plan before it is pulled apart, and leave the plans and doors in the class tray.
