**Setup:** Cut the card doors (two per pair) and stick short strips of masking tape along the edge of each table. Build the teacher doorway for the hook: two towers of LEGO bricks on a base plate with one brick across the top, and two card doors, one taped all the way round and one with no tape.

**Focus:** A hinge joins a door to its frame and lets it swing. Tape works as a hinge because it is flexible: it bends but does not break. The habit this week is **test it, then fix it**.

**Links:** In Lesson 5 students learnt the word hinge and Teddy walked into a house with no door. Today they make the door. In Lesson 7 they build the whole house and add the door they made today.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Recall | Students sit on the mat. Ask: "What does a hinge do?" Think time, then two or three answers. "What did Teddy forget on his house?" (A door.) Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria. |
| 3 to 8 min | Hook (CIC) | Show the teacher doorway with the door taped all the way round. Try to open it: it will not move. "Thumbs up if you think Teddy can get in." Then the door with no tape: it falls off. "Why won't Teddy's door work? Tell a partner." Take two or three ideas. "Today we find out where the tape needs to go." |
| 8 to 13 min | Word wall | Use the word wall. A **frame** is the edges around a door or window. **Attach** means join one thing to another. **Flexible** means it can bend without breaking. Students think first, then each box is uncovered. For each non-example, ask "Why is this not a ___?" and use the reasons below. |
| 13 to 18 min | I do | Think aloud as you build. "First two towers, with the bricks overlapping, like our strong walls. I leave a gap between them for the door. Now one brick across the top: that is the frame. Now I attach the door. I put the tape down one side only, half on the card and half on the brick. The tape is flexible, so it bends." Open and close the door five times while the class counts. |
| 18 to 35 min | You do | Pairs build a doorway on a base plate and attach a door with tape on one side. They open and close it five times. If it sticks or falls off, they fix it. Pairs who finish try a second door with the tape along the top: a flap door, like a pet door. Roam with the questions below. |
| 35 to 40 min | Share | Pairs hold up their doorway. "Point to your hinge." Then, for each tape place, thumbs up or down: one side (yes), the top (yes, a flap), all the way round (no, it cannot swing), no tape (no, it falls off). Say together: **"A hinge joins the door and lets it swing."** |
| 40 to 45 min | Pack up | Each pair puts its best card door in the class tray for Lesson 7. Pull the doorways apart and put the bricks back in the tubs. |

## Word wall: why each non-example is not the word

Say these after students have had a go at explaining.

- **Frame.** A door: it is the part that moves, not the edges around it. A gap with no brick across the top: it has no top edge, so it does not go all the way around. A wall with no gap: there is no opening for a frame to go around.
- **Attach.** A door leaning on the wall: it is touching, but it is not joined. Two bricks side by side but not clicked: they are next to each other, but not joined. A strip of tape on its own: there is only one thing, so nothing is joined.
- **Flexible.** A LEGO brick: it does not bend. An icy pole stick: it bends a little, then it snaps. A wooden block: it does not bend.

## Where to put the tape

| Tape | What happens | A hinge? |
|---|---|---|
| Down one side | The door swings open and shut | Yes |
| Along the top | The door lifts up like a flap | Yes, a flap door |
| All the way round | The door cannot move | No: it is attached, but it cannot swing |
| No tape | The door falls off | No: it is not attached |

## Questions to ask

- Where is your hinge? Show me.
- Why does the tape bend but the brick does not?
- What happens if you tape both sides?
- Does your door stay on when you open it five times?
- Which way does your door swing, in or out?

## What to look for

| Success criterion | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|
| Says what a hinge does | Says it joins the door and lets it swing, or shows it with the door. |
| Attaches a door with tape | Tape on one side only, half on the card and half on the brick. |
| Tests and fixes | Opens the door five times and fixes it if it sticks or falls off. |

## Support and extension

- **Support:** Build the doorway with the pair, then hand them a door with the tape already stuck on the card, ready to press onto the brick.
- **Extension:** Make a window that opens, using the flap hinge, or a door with two hinges (two strips of tape down the same side).

## The key idea

A hinge joins the door to the frame and lets it swing. Tape works as a hinge because it is flexible.

## Notes for the CRT

- Cut the card doors and tear the tape before the class arrives. A doorway 4 studs wide is easier for small hands than a narrow one.
- Keep one good card door per pair in the class tray: students use them in Lesson 7.
- Please pull the doorways apart at the end so the bricks are ready for the next class.
