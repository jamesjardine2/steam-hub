The last lesson of the unit, and a buffer: if Term 4 events take a lesson, this one can be dropped without leaving the game unfinished. It completes the design cycle by evaluating the finished games against the class criteria, and gives the reaction games and panels an audience.

Each pair's game and control panel becomes an **arcade station**. Players rotate through the stations and play **without being told how**, exactly as the design brief asked.

**Optional:** invite a Prep class or buddy class for the arcade rounds. The design brief was "someone who has never played the game, like a Prep student", so real Prep players are the truest test.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Set up the arcade | Pairs set up their game and panel as a station, powered and ready. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria, and the class design criteria from Lesson 5. |
| 5 to 23 min | Arcade rounds | Three rounds of 6 minutes. One partner stays at the station and watches silently (no explaining), the other rotates to play two other games. Swap roles each round. After each game, players leave two sticky notes: one **keep** and one **change**, each linked to a criterion. |
| 23 to 31 min | Final improvement | Pairs read their sticky notes, look for a pattern, choose one change and make it in the code or on the panel. |
| 31 to 39 min | Evaluate | Journal evaluation (below). |
| 39 to 45 min | Share and pack up | Two or three pairs share the most useful feedback they got and what they changed. Components back in kits against the diagram, panels stacked flat, battery packs off. |

## Journal evaluation

1. Which class criterion does your game meet best? What is your evidence?
2. Which criterion was hardest to meet? What would you change next time?
3. Label one input, one output and one variable in your game, and say what each does.

## Write this on the board

- Feedback: **Keep: ___ because ___.** and **Change: ___ because ___.**
- Evaluation stem: **Our game meets "___" because players ___.**

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Could you play this game without anyone explaining it? What helped?
- Which criterion is that sticky note about?
- What pattern do you see in your feedback?
- Where is the variable in your game, and what does it remember?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** give feedback with the criteria list in hand, ticking the ones the game meets.
- **Extension:** take on a new game for the same kit, such as **Simon Says**: the ring flashes a pattern and the player repeats it on the two Jacdac buttons, growing by one each round (it uses a list to remember the pattern).

**Key idea to finish on:** Designers finish by evaluating their solution against the criteria, using what real users did. Feedback from players is what turns a game that works into a game that is good to play.

## Notes for the teacher

- Keep the "no explaining" rule from Lesson 6: the stay-behind partner watches and notes where players get stuck.
- Collect or photograph the journal evaluations as end-of-unit evidence.
- If the unit runs short, this lesson can be dropped; Lesson 6 already includes user testing and one improvement.
