Pairs build the panel they planned in Lesson 5, then find out whether it works by watching other students use it **without saying a word**. A second tester shows whether a problem was a one-off or a real design flaw.

Nothing goes home and nothing needs storing except flat panels: the components come off and go back in the kits.

## Silent test protocol

- The tester gets **no instructions**. The makers say nothing, even if the tester gets stuck.
- Makers record in their journal every time the tester **hesitates, asks, or presses the wrong thing**.
- After the test the tester finishes one sentence: **I was confused when...**

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Revisit | Recap the class criteria from Lesson 5 and model the silent test protocol. Rehearse the no talking rule: it is harder than students expect. |
| 5 to 22 min | Build the panel | On a shoebox lid or A5 card, components attached with Blu Tack or Velcro. Redirect any pair spending time on decoration back to the criteria: "Which problem does that solve?" |
| 22 to 28 min | Silent test, round 1 | Swap panels with another pair. Observers tally each hesitation or mistake in their journal. |
| 28 to 34 min | Silent test, round 2 | A different pair tests. A second tester shows whether a problem was a one-off or a real design flaw. |
| 34 to 41 min | Improve | Make at least one change based on the tests and annotate it in the journal. Fast finishers take on a level up card and update the panel to match. |
| 41 to 45 min | Record and pack up | Photograph the panel for the journal. Components back in kits, panels stacked flat in the class tray. |

## Write this on the board

- The class design criteria from Lesson 5.
- Tester sentence: **I was confused when...**
- Improvement sentence: **We changed ___ because our tester ___.**

## Level up challenges

| Challenge | Code change | Panel change |
|---|---|---|
| Too early | Pressing before green turns the ring red. Add an else to the reaction button's if block. | How will a new player know the rule? Add a warning or instruction. |
| Best time | Make a bestTime variable and update it when a new time is faster. | Where does the best time appear, and how does the player see it? |
| Speed ring | Light more pixels for faster times using set led color at pixel. | Add a key so the player understands what the ring means. |
| Two player | Add a third button. The first player to press after green wins. | Redesign for fairness: equal distance, clear player sides. |

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Which problem does that part of your panel solve?
- Where did your tester hesitate? Was it the same for both testers?
- What is the smallest change that would fix it?
- How will you know your change worked?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** limit the panel to labelling the two buttons and placing the ring, then test.
- **Extension:** a level up card, which changes both the code and what the panel needs to show.

**Key idea to finish on:** Designers test with real users and watch, without helping. Two testers tell us whether a problem is a real design flaw, and each improvement should fix a problem we saw.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Keep the testing silent.** It is the part that keeps fast finishers honest: there is always another tester who gets confused and another improvement to make.
- Test with **at least two different pairs**, not one.
- Photos of the panels and the annotated improvement in the journal are solid Design and Technologies evidence (criteria, generating ideas, testing and evaluating).
