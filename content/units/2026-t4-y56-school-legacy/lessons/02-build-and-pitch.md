**Setup:** before the lesson, put the class trays out and have the numbered iPads ready so each Minecraft and Tinkercad pair gets the one they used in Lesson 1. Write the four design criteria on the board again (or the class's version from Lesson 1). Put a pile of sticky notes on every table.

**Focus:** a design is judged against what it set out to do. The question all lesson is **does it meet the criteria?**

**Links:** Lesson 1 set the brief and the criteria and ended with a sketch and a build under way. This lesson finishes the build and uses the criteria to give feedback. If Lesson 1 was the only lesson a class had, their Design Cards are still a complete record of a design idea.

## How the gallery walk works

- **Leave it ready:** each pair leaves their build on their table with the Design Card next to it. iPad pairs leave the design open on screen.
- **Move:** pairs move one table clockwise. In a small room, one pair moves while the other pair at that table stays seated, then they swap.
- **Read, look, write:** read the Design Card first, then look at the design. Write two stars and a wish on sticky notes and stick them on the card.
- **Two rounds:** about 4 minutes at each design, then move again.
- **Hands off:** look, don't touch. iPads stay where they are.

**Key words:** design criteria, feature, feedback, evaluate, improve

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and recall | Students sit facing you. Pairs tell each other: "Who is your user? What do they need?" Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 21 min | Finish the build | Builds and iPads come back out. Pairs finish their design. At 12 minutes, say: "Three features on your card now." Pairs write three features and how each helps the user, then tick the criteria check. Roam with the questions below. |
| 21 to 24 min | Model feedback | Back to the buddy bench example. Model: "Star: it helps the user, because the picture sign means a Foundation child can find it without reading. Star: it fits the place, because the tree gives shade at lunch. Wish: it could last longer if it were made of recycled plastic, not wood." Point to the criterion for each one. |
| 24 to 34 min | Gallery walk | Pairs leave their designs ready. Two rounds of about 4 minutes, moving one table clockwise each time. Every sticky note names a criterion. |
| 34 to 38 min | Read and improve | Pairs go back to their own design, read the sticky notes, and write one improvement on their card. If there is time, they make it. |
| 38 to 42 min | Pitch | Two or three pairs pitch in 30 seconds using the stem: "Our design is ___. It is for ___. It helps them by ___." |
| 42 to 45 min | Pack up | Photograph each design with its card if you are making the Book Creator book. LEGO back in the tubs. Paper models go home or on display. Design Cards are glued into STEAM journals. |

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Which feature helps your user the most? How do you know?
- Which criterion is your design weakest on? What could fix it?
- If the principal said yes tomorrow, what would it be made of?
- What would your user say if they saw this?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** a pair can write one feature instead of three. During the gallery walk, give them the sentence stems on a card: "Star: it ___ because ___." "Wish: it could ___ so that ___."
- **Extension:** write a short pitch to the principal with the design's cost, materials and where it would go. Or redesign one feature using a sticky note's wish and explain the change.

**Key idea to finish on:** designers test their ideas against the criteria and use feedback to make them better.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Small room:** the gallery walk works best if half the pairs move while the other half stay as hosts. Swap after one round.
- **Minecraft:** pairs can put signs in their world to label the three features, as well as writing them on the card.
- **Book Creator:** a one-page-per-design book makes a good gift for the principal and a record for Year 6 graduation. Photograph the designs and cards only, with no faces, and cover names on the cards before photographing them.
- **Assessment:** the Design Card shows the user, the need, three features linked to the criteria and one improvement. That is the evidence for Design and Technologies reports.
