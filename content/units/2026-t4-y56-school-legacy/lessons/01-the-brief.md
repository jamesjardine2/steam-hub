**Setup:** before the lesson, take four or five photos of school spaces with no people in them, ready to show on the board after the hook question. Put the four tools in four zones of the room so pairs can move to theirs without crowding: LEGO on one table, paper and card on another, and iPads at the desks. Have trays labelled for each class for builds that stay until Lesson 2. In a small room with 30 or more students, keep everyone seated for the brief and the worked example, and only start moving once every pair has a sketch.

**Users:** Year 6 can choose any user at FCPS. Year 5 design for the Foundation children who start at FCPS in 2027, so every Year 5 pair has the same user and chooses a place and a need.

**Focus:** designers start with the user, not the object. The question all lesson is **who is it for, and what do they need?**

## The brief

- **The problem:** design something for FCPS that helps a real user after you have gone.
- **Choose a user:** Foundation children, the whole school, students who need a quiet space, or the garden and the animals that use it. Year 5: Foundation children starting in 2027.
- **Choose a place:** a real place at school, such as the junior playground, the garden, a corridor or the oval.
- **Design for a need:** name one thing the user needs that the place does not give them yet.
- **Choose a tool:** LEGO, paper and card, Tinkercad or Minecraft Education. Pairs keep the same tool for both lessons.

**Key words:** legacy, user, need, design criteria, feature, sustainable

## Design criteria

Show these four on the board and keep them up all lesson. Ask pairs to say why each one matters. If the class has a better idea, swap one or add a fifth.

1. **It helps our user.** It meets the need we named.
2. **It fits the place.** It suits the size and use of the real space.
3. **It is safe.** No one could get hurt using it.
4. **It lasts and wastes little.** It would survive school life and use materials wisely.

## Lesson sequence

| Time | Step | What to do and say |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Settle and focus | Students sit facing you. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. |
| 3 to 8 min | Hook (CIC) | Show the hook slide and ask: "What should FCPS have after you've gone?" Then show your photos of school spaces. Take two or three answers. Then introduce the word **legacy**: something you leave behind that helps the people who come after you. |
| 8 to 15 min | The brief and the user | Read the brief slide. Show the user choices. Pairs choose a user and a place. Turn and talk: "Our user needs ___ because ___." Year 5: "What did you need when you started in Foundation?" Take three answers. |
| 15 to 20 min | Design criteria | Show the four criteria. For each, ask a pair to explain why it matters. Ask: "Is there anything we should swap or add?" Agree on the list and leave it on the board. |
| 20 to 25 min | Worked example | Fill in a Design Card on the board while thinking aloud: "My user is Foundation children. My place is the junior playground. They need somewhere to go when they have no one to play with. My design is a buddy bench. Feature 1: it is a bright colour, so it is easy to find, and that helps my user. Feature 2: a sign with a picture, because many Foundation children can't read yet. Feature 3: it sits under a tree for shade, so it fits the place." Point to the criterion each feature meets. |
| 25 to 31 min | Sketch | Pairs fill in the user, place and need on their Design Card, then sketch their design and label three features. Check every pair can say what the user needs before they move on. |
| 31 to 41 min | Choose and build | Pairs tick their tool on the card and start building. Roam with the questions below. |
| 41 to 45 min | Save and pack up | LEGO and paper builds go on the class tray with the Design Card. Minecraft pairs save the world with both first names. Tinkercad pairs check the design has saved. iPad pairs write their iPad number on the card. Cards are collected. |

## Questions to ask while roaming

- Who is your user? What do they need that they don't have now?
- Which criterion does this feature meet?
- How big would this be in real life? Would it fit the place?
- What would happen to it after a year of school life?

## Support and extension

- **Support:** give a pair the buddy bench example to change for a different user or place. Let them start building straight from a quick sketch.
- **Extension:** ask the pair to add a fourth feature that solves a problem a second user would have, without making it worse for the first user.

**Key idea to finish on:** good designers start with the user. Every feature should help the person it is for.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Tools:** LEGO and paper builds stay on trays until Lesson 2. Minecraft Education worlds save on the iPad they were made on, so a pair needs the same iPad next week. [Check Tinkercad sign-in on the iPads: a class code or student accounts.]
- **Minecraft:** a flat world gives pairs a clear site. Remind pairs it is one design, not a whole town.
- **Paper:** card folded into boxes and tubes is enough for most designs. Keep scissors and tape at the paper table.
- **Privacy:** the hook photos should show spaces only, with no students in them.
- Keep the worked example on the board, or take a photo of it, for Lesson 2.
