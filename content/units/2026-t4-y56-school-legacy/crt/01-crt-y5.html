Lesson 1: The Brief

Year 5 STEAM | Unit: Legacy Project | CRT overview | 45 minutes

No preparation and no screens. Pairs design something for the Foundation children who start at our school next year, make a quick sketch on scrap paper, then build a paper model of it. Everyone uses paper, scrap card, scissors and sticky tape. Please keep the models on a tray or spare table: next lesson the pairs finish them.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To find out what a user at our school needs and start a design that meets that need.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • describe who my design is for and what they need.
  • explain why each of our class design criteria matters.
  • sketch a design with three labelled features and start building it.

The brief

Design something for our school that helps the Foundation children who start next year. Each pair chooses a real place at school and one thing a new Foundation child needs there.

PlacesNeeds a new Foundation child might have
The junior playgroundSomewhere to go when they have no one to play with
The school entranceHelp saying goodbye to their family in the morning
The corridorsFinding their way to the toilets, the office or their room
The garden or the ovalShade, a drink, or a calm place to sit

Write the four design criteria on the board: It helps our user. It fits the place. It is safe. It lasts and wastes little. A user is the person the design is for. A legacy is something you leave behind that helps the people who come after you.

The lesson in brief

TimeWhat to do
0 to 7 minAsk: "Think back to your first day in Foundation. What was hard? What did you need?" Take three answers. Say: "Next year, new Foundation children start here. You are going to design something that helps them. That is your legacy." Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria.
7 to 13 minRead the brief and the places. Pairs choose a place and a need, then turn and talk: "Our user needs ___ because ___." Take three answers.
13 to 19 minRead the four criteria. Ask a pair why each one matters. Then think aloud with an example: "A buddy bench for the junior playground. It is a bright colour so it is easy to find. It has a picture sign, because Foundation children can't read much yet. It sits in the shade." Point to the criterion each feature meets.
19 to 24 minSketch: hand out scrap paper. A quick sketch with three features labelled 1, 2 and 3 (not kept). Check each pair can say what their user needs.
24 to 40 minBuild: helpers hand out 3 sheets of paper and scissors per pair, scrap card if there is any, and tape for each table. Pairs build a paper model. At about 32 minutes: "Hands off. Thumbs for each criterion. Fix your weakest part first."
40 to 45 minBoth first names in pencil on the base of each model, models onto the tray. 2 or 3 pairs share: "Our design is ___. It helps a Foundation child by ___."
Key idea to finish on: Good designers start with the user, not the object. Every feature should help the person it is for. Next lesson we finish the models and give each other feedback using the four criteria.

Notes for the teacher

  • Pairs often jump to something fun for themselves (a waterslide, a games room). Ask: "How does this help a five-year-old on their first day?"
  • Keep it to one design, not a whole playground. Scissors stay on the table.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
