Lesson 1: The Brief

Year 6 STEAM | Unit: Legacy Project | CRT plan | 45 minutes

No preparation needed. Student helpers hand everything out during the lesson. No screens are used.

This is the first of two lessons. Each pair plans a design for the school on one page of their STEAM journal, then starts a paper model of it. The models are kept for next lesson. Journals are on the shelves as you walk in the door.

Learning Intention and Success Criteria

Learning Intention:

To find out what a user at our school needs and start a design that meets that need.

Success Criteria:

I can:

  • describe who my design is for and what they need.
  • explain why each of our class design criteria matters.
  • sketch a design with three labelled features and start building it.

Read these aloud at the start and come back to them at the end.

How the design task works

  • The brief: design something for the school that helps a real user after Year 6 have left. This is their legacy.
  • Choose a user: Foundation children, the whole school, students who need a quiet space, or the garden and its animals.
  • Choose a place: a real place at school, such as the junior playground, the garden, a corridor or the oval.
  • Name a need: one thing the user needs that the place does not give them yet.
  • Plan in the journal: each student sets out a page with the date, the title Legacy Project, then User, Place and Need, a sketch with three features labelled 1, 2 and 3, and one line for each feature saying how it helps the user. Partners plan the same design.
  • Build: pairs make a paper model of their design. Fold paper into strips, tubes and boxes, cut and tape. Write both first names in pencil on the base.

Key words

legacy, user, need, design criteria, feature, sustainable

Lesson sequence

TimeStepWhat to do and say
0 to 7 minSettle and hookStudents sit facing you. Read the Learning Intention and Success Criteria aloud. Ask: "Close your eyes and picture the junior playground, or the garden. What should our school have after you've gone?" Take two or three answers. Write legacy on the board and say: "A legacy is something you leave behind that helps the people who come after you."
7 to 13 minThe brief and the userWrite the brief on the board: Design something for our school that helps a real user. Read out the four users and some places (see How the design task works). Pairs choose a user and a place, then turn and talk using the sentence stem on the board: "Our user needs ___ because ___." Take three answers (for example, "Foundation children need somewhere to go when they have no one to play with").
13 to 21 minCriteria and exampleWrite the four design criteria on the board (below). Ask a pair to say why each one matters ("Why does it need to fit the place?" So it is the right size and suits how the space is used). Then draw a bench under a tree on the board and think aloud: "My user is Foundation children. My place is the junior playground. They need somewhere to go when they have no one to play with. My design is a buddy bench. Feature 1: a bright colour, so it is easy to find. Feature 2: a sign with a picture, because many Foundation children can't read yet. Feature 3: it sits under a tree for shade." Point to the criterion each feature meets.
21 to 29 minPlan in journalsHelpers hand out journals, pencils and rulers. Each student sets out the page (date, Legacy Project, User, Place, Need), sketches the design and labels three features. Check every pair can say what their user needs before they move on.
29 to 41 minBuildHelpers hand out 3 sheets of paper and 1 pair of scissors per pair, scrap card if there is any, and a roll of tape per table. Pairs build a paper model of their design. Roam with the questions below.
41 to 45 minShare and pack upPack up first: names on the base of each model in pencil, then models onto the tray or spare table. Scraps in the recycling. Two or three pairs share their design using the sentence stem. Finish with the key idea (below). Journals back onto the shelves.

Write this on the board

Design criteriaWhat it means
It helps our user.It meets the need we named.
It fits the place.It suits the size and use of the real space.
It is safe.No one could get hurt using it.
It lasts and wastes little.It would survive school life and use materials wisely.

Sentence stems: Our user needs ______ because ______. and Feature ___ helps our user because ______.

Questions to ask while roaming

  • Who is your user? What do they need that they don't have now?
  • Which criterion does this feature meet?
  • How big would this be in real life? Would it fit the place?
  • What would happen to it after a year of school life?

Support and extension

WhoWhat to do
SupportLet the pair start from the buddy bench example and change it for a different user or place. They can write one feature instead of three and start building from a quick sketch.
ExtensionAdd a fourth feature that helps a second user without making it worse for the first. Write one sentence explaining the choice.
Key idea to finish on: Good designers start with the user, not the object. Every feature should help the person it is for. Next lesson we finish the designs and give each other feedback using our four criteria.

Notes for the teacher

  • Common mistake: pairs jump to a fun object (a waterslide, a pool) without a user or a need. Ask: "Who is it for, and what do they need?" before they build.
  • Keep it to one design. Some pairs try to design a whole playground. Ask them to choose the one part that helps their user most.
  • Safety: scissors are for cutting only and stay on the table.
  • Please keep the models and journals. Next lesson starts by finishing the models, and the journal page is each pair's design plan.
  • Please leave a short note about how far the class got and any students who were absent.
